## Mosaic chart 

The mosaic chart visually represents the "relationship" between the two chosen categorical (factor) variables using rectangles. Examining the sizes and positions of the rectangles helps understand the distribution and interaction of the categorical variables.

The Rectangles represent combinations of the levels of each variable. Sometimes the levels are the same, sometimes they are different.
The area of each rectangle is proportional to the frequency of the combination. Larger rectangles indicate more frequent category combinations, revealing patterns and relationships between variables.
Levels of one variable are shown along one axis, and levels of the other variable are shown along the other axis.
If visible, the shading represents the strength of the correlation of the pairs of levels. 
Blue is a positive correlation, red is a negative correlation; the darker the shade, the stronger the correlation. These correlations only suggest potential relationships in the data; some correlations may be coincidental.
